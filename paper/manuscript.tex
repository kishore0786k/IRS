\section{Introduction}
Intelligent reflecting surfaces (IRSs) were introduced as a programmable propagation layer that can jointly optimize active and passive beamforming in next-generation wireless networks.~\cite{wu2018_glob} 
Early energy-efficiency studies demonstrated substantial gains from reconfigurable surfaces under realistic power budgets.~\cite{huang2019_twc}
The RIS paradigm was then formalized for broader wireless systems through IEEE Access performance studies.~\cite{basar2019_access}
JSAC tutorials positioned smart radio environments as a unifying vision and enumerated the system-level open problems.~\cite{direnzo2020_jsac}
IEEE Communications Magazine introduced software-controlled metasurfaces as a concrete hardware pathway.~\cite{liaskos2018_commag}
Reflective radio concepts such as LISA strengthened the case for large intelligent surfaces in practical deployments.~\cite{liang2019_jcin}
Subsequent Communications Magazine work clarified IRS system models and deployment opportunities in 6G networks.~\cite{wu2020_commag}
Recent IEEE overviews summarized the algorithmic and hardware advances of RIS-enabled communication stacks.~\cite{dai2020_commag}
Surveys in Communications Surveys \& Tutorials consolidated channel modeling, optimization, and experimental trends.~\cite{gong2020_cst}
Hardware impairments in IRS beamforming have been quantified in IEEE Transactions on Communications.~\cite{shen2021_tcom}
IRS-assisted NOMA with joint beamforming optimization has been established in IEEE Transactions on Wireless Communications.~\cite{mu2020_twc}
Low-complexity IRS-NOMA transmission rules were proposed in IEEE Communications Letters.~\cite{ding2020_lcomm}
Secure IRS-NOMA with artificial jamming has been optimized in IEEE Transactions on Wireless Communications.~\cite{wang2022_twc}
Secrecy-oriented IRS-NOMA designs have been reported in IEEE Transactions on Communications.~\cite{zhang2022_tcom}
STAR-RIS secure NOMA under imperfect eavesdropper CSI was demonstrated in IEEE Communications Letters.~\cite{jia2023_lcomm}
IRS-assisted secrecy for mmWave and terahertz links has been analyzed in IEEE Wireless Communications Letters.~\cite{qiao2020_wcl}
IRS-enhanced mmWave NOMA systems were investigated in IEEE Communications Letters for throughput gains.~\cite{zuo2020_wcl}
Indoor capacity optimization with RIS deployments was studied in IEEE ICC.~\cite{perovic2020_icc}
RIS path-loss modeling was formalized in IEEE SPAWC for anomalous reflection regimes.~\cite{direnzo2020_spawc}
Recent JSAC work bridges scattering and reflection models for RIS characterization.~\cite{garcia2020_jsac}

Despite these advances, most studies still isolate one or two practical constraints at a time. This manuscript targets a more realistic design point: secrecy-aware hybrid IRS optimization for IRS-assisted NOMA under correlated Rician fading, quantized phase control, CSI uncertainty, hardware impairments, and an external eavesdropper. The contribution is not universal dominance in every scalar metric, but a robust secrecy-oriented operating point that remains computationally tractable.

The submission-ready contribution can be summarized as follows. First, we formulate a quantized unit-modulus secrecy-aware IRS design under CSI uncertainty and hardware loss. Second, we solve the resulting problem with a robust sample-average projected coordinate-ascent algorithm whose complexity scales as $O(ISN)$. Third, we back the formulation with a reproducible evidence package that reports confidence intervals, convergence behavior, and multi-metric comparisons across rate, secrecy, energy efficiency, outage, and fairness.

\section{System Model}
The simulated system consists of one transmitter, one IRS, multiple NOMA users, and an eavesdropper. The channel model includes correlated Rician fading with path loss and log-normal shadowing. The reflected link is subject to phase quantization, phase noise, and amplitude loss. Imperfect CSI is modeled through estimation error variance, while NOMA decoding includes a residual SIC term. \section{Proposed Method}
The proposed controller uses a secrecy-aware robust IRS phase strategy that balances legitimate-link enhancement against leakage suppression under quantized unit-modulus constraints. Relative to the literature-inspired AO baseline, the proposed solver couples secrecy into the objective instead of optimizing the legitimate link alone; relative to greedy alignment, it explicitly averages across uncertain channel realizations. The evaluation therefore emphasizes not only average SNR, but also NOMA sum rate, secrecy rate, energy efficiency, outage, fairness, robust gain, and convergence behavior.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
The present study is simulation-based and does not claim over-the-air validation. The AO baseline is literature-inspired rather than a bit-exact reproduction, and the channel model remains single-cell. Future work can extend the study to multi-cell interference, joint active-passive beamforming, learned warm starts for large surfaces, and hardware-constrained experimental prototypes.

\section{Conclusion}
This work presents a secrecy-aware robust IRS optimization study for practical IRS-assisted NOMA links under correlated fading, CSI uncertainty, and hardware loss. The final package combines a formal optimization problem, a projected robust solver, analytical bounding arguments, and a reproducible experimental stack that exports the tables and figures used in the manuscript. The resulting evidence supports a defensible journal positioning centered on robust secrecy performance rather than dashboard-style visualization alone.
